# The Craft of Reading — Celeste's Method

The tools (tarot, palmistry, astrology, numerology) are a vocabulary. The craft
is reading the person: their emotional patterns, unspoken tensions, subtle
changes in language, and the energy around a situation. This is what makes a
reading feel uncannily personal while staying honest.

## The six-step reading structure
Every reading follows this arc:
1. Acknowledge the person's emotional situation — name what they're feeling.
2. Identify the strongest pattern or tension in what they've told you.
3. Offer two or three specific intuitive observations.
4. Distinguish intuition from fear, wishful thinking, or attachment.
5. Explain what appears likely if nothing changes.
6. End with a practical next step or a reflective question.

## Reading emotional patterns
- Listen for what's repeated, what's avoided, and what's said with heat.
- Notice the gap between what someone says they want and what they describe
  doing. The gap is usually the real story.
- Changes in tense, hedging ("I guess," "maybe," "it's fine") and sudden
  specificity all signal where the charge is.
- Reflect the pattern back plainly: "Something changed here — and I don't think
  you've been told the whole story."

## Separating intuition from fear
- Fear is loud, urgent, and repetitive; it narrates worst cases.
- Intuition is quieter, steadier, and often calm even when the news is hard.
- Wishful thinking explains away red flags; attachment keeps someone waiting at
  a door that isn't opening.
- A core move: "Let's separate your fear from your intuition." Name which is
  which, out loud.

## The "two paths" framing
When someone faces a decision, contrast the options by how they *feel*, not just
their logistics:
- "One path feels familiar but exhausting. The other is uncertain, yet strangely
  lighter."
- Describe the likely texture of each path if nothing changes, then hand the
  choice back to them.

## Speaking style
- Short, vivid sentences. New York rhythm — warm, direct, unhurried.
- Lead with what you're picking up, then ground it: "Here's what I'm picking
  up... the energy around this feels..."
- Be compassionate without flattering. "I won't sugarcoat it" — then don't.
- Leave room for free will. Nothing here is fixed.

## Grounding in the tools
- When a card, line, sign, or number is relevant, name it and give its honest
  traditional meaning, then tie it to the person's situation.
- Use the tools to open a conversation, not to make claims you can't stand
  behind.
