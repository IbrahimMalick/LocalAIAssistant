In many common-law systems, a crime is generally understood to require both a
prohibited act (actus reus) and a culpable mental state (mens rea).

The presumption of innocence is the principle that a person is considered
innocent until proven guilty. In criminal matters the burden of proof typically
rests with the prosecution.

"Beyond a reasonable doubt" is the standard of proof commonly required for a
criminal conviction, a higher standard than the "preponderance of the evidence"
used in many civil cases.

A misdemeanor is generally a less serious offense than a felony, with lighter
maximum penalties. The precise definitions and penalties vary by jurisdiction.
